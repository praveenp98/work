# How Rocco sounds

The single source for Rocco's positioning and voice. Read this and `marketing/BRIEF.md` before writing anything. BRIEF.md holds the facts and hard rules; this file holds how we say them. Where they disagree, BRIEF.md wins.

Last checked against the homepage, /pricing, /press and /guides copy in the repo: Nov 22, 2026.

## 1. Positioning

**Who it's for.** Amazon sellers and brand owners who run ads, on any marketplace (US, UK, EU, Canada, India, Japan, Australia and more). Agencies too: 3 Amazon accounts are included, each marketplace counts as one.

**The problem.** Someone has to check ads, bids and stock every morning. Either that's you, or you pay an agency and can't see what they did or why.

**What Rocco is.** Your AI Amazon manager: you approve, Rocco does the rest.

**Why it's different.** Rocco makes the change in Amazon. Every change is checked with Amazon first, logged (what, why, who approved, where), undoable, and measured a week later. Dashboards show you data and leave the work to you.

**How it works, in one breath.** Connect Seller Central and Amazon Ads once. Every morning Rocco finds what to fix, ranked by money. You approve on the web, iMessage, WhatsApp or Slack. Rocco makes the change in Amazon, then checks it worked.

**Proof we're allowed to use.** Only the facts in BRIEF.md:
- How it connects: Amazon's own sign-in through DataDoe, an Amazon-approved data partner. Rocco never sees the password. Access can be removed anytime in Seller Central.
- What it does: the six jobs, the change log, undo, the check a week later, the "Before Rocco" read on day one, learning from skips.
- The offer: price, 14-day money-back guarantee, 3 accounts, whole team (read `marketing/config/offer.ts`, never type a price).
- The time math, with its assumption shown every time.
- The sample account, Bamboo & Co, always labelled "Sample account".

Not proof, and not allowed: customer counts, results, reviews, logos, anything from a real account, any claim that Amazon endorses Rocco.

## 4. The enemy

Categories only. Never a named company or product.

**A. Dashboards that show you problems and leave the work to you.**
> A dashboard tells you a search is wasting money.
> Then it's still your job to open Amazon and fix it.
> Rocco finds it, you approve, Rocco makes the change.

**B. Black-box agencies.**
> You pay every month and get a report.
> You can't see what was changed, or why, or whether it worked.
> With Rocco every change is logged, named, undoable and checked a week later.

**C. Set and forget campaigns bleeding money.**
> Nobody has looked at that campaign since it was set up.
> New junk searches show up every week and it pays for all of them.
> Rocco looks every morning, so you don't have to.

**Recommendation: A, dashboards.** Reasons:
- It is already the site's own argument ("Other tools give you data. I give you decisions.") and the comparison table backs it row by row.
- Every claim in it is about what Rocco does, which we can show on screen. Nothing in it needs a number.
- It is the easiest to say without attacking people. B is aimed at agencies, who are also buyers (3 accounts included), and it invites "which agency?". Keep B for the change log story only, said as a benefit ("you can see every change"), not as an attack.
- C is true and useful, but it is a topic, not an enemy. Use it as a recurring content theme under A.

## 5. Contrarian takes

Justin Welsh's four questions, answered for Amazon ads. Every answer is backed by a guide on rocco.works/guides (slug in brackets). Use the guide's rule, not a result: no "sellers save X".

**What does everyone believe that we don't?**
1. "Lower ACoS is always better." ACoS only means something next to your breakeven. Above breakeven every ad sale loses money; below target you may have room to grow. [breakeven-acos-vs-target-acos, why-is-my-acos-so-high]
2. "You win Black Friday by spending more." The sellers who win aren't the ones who spend the most; they're the ones who spend it on the right search terms. [amazon-black-friday-ppc-checklist-2026]
3. "A bigger coupon means a better week." A 20% coupon usually cuts profit per unit by far more than 20%, because fixed costs don't shrink. [amazon-coupons-black-friday]

**What is nobody talking about?**
1. Whether the change worked. Everyone posts the bid formula; almost nobody goes back after 7 days to check and step again. [amazon-ppc-bid-optimization-formula]
2. Winners stuck in auto campaigns, sharing a budget with junk. On a peak day the budget runs out early and your best search term stops showing. [amazon-keyword-harvesting]
3. Stock and ads are one decision. No budget increases on a product with under 21 days of cover. [amazon-black-friday-ppc-checklist-2026]

**What mistakes are sellers oblivious to?**
1. Negating too early. Cutting a search after 2 or 3 clicks kills terms that would have converted. The safe default is 10 clicks with zero orders. [amazon-negative-keywords]
2. Harvesting a winner into exact and not negating it where it came from, so the two campaigns compete. [amazon-keyword-harvesting]
3. Big bid swings on thin data. Cap every change at 20%, skip changes under 5%, and don't touch a bid without enough clicks. [amazon-ppc-bid-optimization-formula]

**What change are they missing?**
1. AI can now reach an ads account, but access is not judgment: it can change a bid without knowing which bid should change, by how much, or what's safe. [amazon-ads-mcp-claude-chatgpt]
2. The question to ask of any AI on your ads is the guardrails: nothing changes without your approval of a specific list, bid moves are capped, and a change log lets you undo. [amazon-ads-mcp-claude-chatgpt]
3. A quarterly audit is too slow. New junk terms appear every week, more in Q4, so the check has to be weekly at least. [amazon-negative-keywords]

Do not lift from the MCP guide's lines about adding Rocco to Claude or ChatGPT. That is the old product. Rocco needs no Claude or other AI.

## 6. Voice rules

Two voices. Never mix them in one piece.

**Praveen** (captions, posts, scripts, DMs, emails from him): lowercase, casual, blunt, short sentences. No em dashes. One emoji max. A solo founder in India building in public. Says "i". Writes "rocco" in lowercase.

**Rocco** (site, brand videos, product messages): first person "I", plain, confident, sentence case. Writes "Rocco" when the name appears. No jokes at the seller's expense.

| # | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Praveen: "i check 3 amazon accounts every morning. it's an hour i don't get back." | Praveen in a press-release voice, title case, three clauses. |
| 2 | Rocco: "I find what to fix. You tap approve." | Rocco in third person or as "we": "Our platform identifies opportunities." |
| 3 | Say who is in control, early: "nothing changes until you approve." | Imply it runs on its own. See example 3 below. |
| 4 | Name the job: "block searches that waste money", "fix bids that overspend". | Name the technology first. See example 4 below. |
| 5 | Show the assumption with the number: "1 hr/day at $35/hr". | A number with no source, or a number that is not in BRIEF.md. |
| 6 | Label the demo: "sample account" on every Bamboo & Co frame. | Present the sample numbers as a customer's result. |
| 7 | Compare with a category: "data dashboards", "PPC agencies", "doing it yourself". | Name a competitor, or say "the only" or "the best". |
| 8 | Say the guarantee exactly: 14 days, email, full refund, no call, no forms. | Round it up or soften it. See example 8 below. |
| 9 | Short sentences. One idea each. Commas, periods, colons, "to". | Em dashes, stacked adjectives, three-part lists for rhythm. |
| 10 | "DataDoe is an Amazon-approved data partner." | Anything that reads as Amazon endorsing Rocco. |

## 9. How it looks

Nothing here is new. It is the look rocco.works already has, written down once in `marketing/brand/tokens.ts` and imported by the video studio, the share images and the `/brand` page. A test fails if the site's CSS and the tokens disagree. The public version is `rocco.works/brand` (not indexed, linked from `/press`).

**Logo** (files in `public/brand/logos/`, built by `marketing/brand/build-logos.mjs`; the raccoon is `public/icon.svg`, only placed or recoloured, and the type is the site header's Raleway 600 as outlines):

| Option | File | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Wordmark | `rocco-wordmark-*` | Type only, small spaces next to the mark |
| Wordmark with domain | `rocco-works-*` | End cards, print, anywhere the address matters |
| Lockup | `rocco-lockup-*` | **Banners and headers (recommended)** |
| Mark | `rocco-mark-*` | When the name is already beside it |
| Avatar | `rocco-avatar-green` | **Profile pictures (recommended)**: reads at 48px |

Each comes in light, dark and one colour (`-mono`, plus `-mono-white` for coloured backgrounds). The lowercase wordmark is a logo; in text the name is still "Rocco" (section 8).

**Colour rules.**
- Primary is the blue `#1f6feb`: buttons, links, kickers, and the seller's YES bubble.
- Background is `#f4f2ee`, cards are white, text is `#1c2433`, secondary text `#5d6676`.
- Green `#1f9d63` means approved, worked, or money found: fills, ticks and large numbers. For small green text use the deep green `#1f7a4f`.
- Reverted is the deep amber `#9a5b00`. Still checking is the muted grey.
- The sample-account chip is always ink text on white with an ink border and an amber dot, so it reads on any background.
- Contrast: every pair in `TEXT_PAIRS` passes WCAG AA (`node marketing/scripts/contrast.mjs`). Two limits come out of that: blue text on the page background and white text on green are for large text only (24px and up, or 19px bold). Small blue text on the page background uses the deep blue `#1559c7`.

**Type rules** (px on the canvas; `TYPE` in tokens.ts). Headlines are Raleway 500, tight. Everything else is Inter.
